## The number, in one paragraph

If you visit the Pentagon's casualty page for this war, the one labeled Operation Epic Fury, you will see 417 wounded. That page stopped growing around July 7, 2026. Since then the government has been adding casualties to a second page, labeled "Overseas Operations," which as of September 2 showed 350 more wounded. The two pages never appear together. Added up, the official count of wounded in action is 767, and the official count of deaths is 18. Our own estimate of every US service member medically significantly injured in this war is a floor of about 965, a central band of about 1,000 to 1,150, and a ceiling of about 1,300. Our estimate of deaths is 20: the official 18 plus two publicly documented non-hostile deaths that appear on neither page.

## Why the public page shows 417 when the government's own total is 767

This is the largest single piece of the gap, and it is not concealment. It is filing. The Pentagon opened the second page in early July and kept the first one frozen. Anyone who reads only the war's named page sees 46 percent of the government's own total. We pulled both pages live on September 3, 2026, and archived them; a national outlet, The Intercept, had reported the second page's existence on August 4. Two independent routes, same fact.

## What we count that the government does not

The official pages count "wounded in action," which by the rules means hostile injury. They have no category at all for sailors hurt in an accident. Roughly 200 sailors were treated after the fire aboard the USS Gerald R. Ford; the Navy published that figure; none of them are on either casualty page, because the database has no place to put them. That definitional gap, about 200, is the second-largest piece of the difference between the public number and ours. Two smaller pieces are a modeled 30 to 180 injuries whose diagnosis arrives late (chiefly brain injuries from blast), measured on this war's own disclosure pattern rather than borrowed from an older one, and a set of documented omissions, including the two deaths above, that are small in size but are the only part of the gap that suggests the rules were broken rather than followed.

## How sure we are, in words

High confidence on the floor, because it is the government's own two pages added to the Navy's own Ford figure. Moderate confidence on the central band. Low confidence on the ceiling. High confidence on 20 deaths. The most fragile single element is the Ford figure: if "treated" turns out to mean mostly smoke inhalation with a return to duty the same watch, and only 20 to 50 of those sailors were seriously hurt, our floor falls from about 965 to about 800. We say that here because a reader deciding whether to trust this page should know where it would break first.

## The strongest case against our own number

We wrote it ourselves, and it is the part of our working estimate to read first if you are skeptical. In short: the Ford figure may be counted too generously; adding the two pages assumes no person is listed on both, which we cannot prove without personal data we will not seek; our measured "late diagnosis" curve may be measuring the Pentagon's paperwork backlog rather than injuries; and the Pentagon's spokesman says 96 percent of the wounded returned to duty and most injuries were minor concussions, which, if true, means "over a thousand wounded" describes something materially different from what happened to most of those people. Our number is a count of injured persons, and we say so every time we state it.

## How this count was made, and what we will not do

Every figure above comes from one of three places: the two official casualty pages, pulled live by our own script every night and archived on sight; published figures from the military itself (the Navy's Ford number, the spokesman's statements); and reporting by named outlets that we could reproduce to the digit against our own captures. No Iranian claim is counted; all were logged and set aside. No wounded person is named anywhere in this work. We have contacted no families, joined no private groups, and filed no records request that names an individual. The dead are named only because they are on the public memorial roll.
