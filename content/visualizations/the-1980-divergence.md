## What Happened

Around 1979, the lines diverged. Productivity kept climbing: workers got better, faster, more efficient. But pay for most workers grew far more slowly: from the end of 1979 to the middle of 2026, what an hour of work produces rose 93.7 percent, while a typical worker's hourly pay rose 32.7 percent. The gap between what Americans produce and what they earn has been growing for more than 45 years. That shaded area isn't an abstraction. It's wealth that was generated by workers and captured by something else.

The causes are well-documented: union decline, shareholder primacy, tax policy shifts, offshoring, automation without reinvestment. The result is a population that's more productive than ever and less secure than their grandparents.

Foundation exists because of this chart. The 16 components (healthcare, housing, food, education, UBI) aren't new spending. They're what happens when you redirect even a fraction of the divergence back toward the people who generated it.
