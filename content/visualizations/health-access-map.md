Staying healthy starts before the doctor's office. It depends on whether you have health insurance, and on whether you can get to a store that sells fresh food. This map shows both, state by state.

## What you're looking at

**Uninsured rate** is the share of people under 65 who had no health insurance in 2024. Nationally it was 9.8 percent. In plain words: about one in ten people under 65 had no coverage at all.

**Food access** is the share of each state's people who are both low-income and live far from a supermarket: more than a mile away in a town or city, more than ten miles in the country. The U.S. Department of Agriculture calls these low-income, low-access areas, often known as food deserts. These figures are from 2019, the most recent full count.

## What the two maps show together

States where more people have no insurance also tend to be states where more low-income people live far from a supermarket. The match is strong but not perfect, and a map cannot say why. Oklahoma expanded Medicaid on July 1, 2021, and its uninsured rate is still among the highest in the country.

Foundation treats [health care](/foundation/freedom-from/healthcare/) and [food](/foundation/freedom-from/food/) as parts of one floor, because a person who lacks one often lacks the other.
