Oklahoma has more than 20,000 known abandoned oil and gas wells, and the state says the true number is higher. An unplugged well can leak methane or let oil and salt water reach groundwater, and when no company is left to plug it, the job falls to the state. The Oklahoma Corporation Commission ranks the wells by risk and plugs the worst first. Its rough estimate to plug the known wells is about $575 million, and it suggests planning for about twice that. The wells earn nothing.

## Phoenix Wells

Toggle "Show Phoenix conversion" to see the idea. Some wells get capped, which ends the risk. Others get converted to geothermal power, owned by the communities that host them. The same workforce that drilled them maintains them. The same land hosts them. The same ingenuity that built the oil industry powers the next one.

Hit "Animate" to watch one possible conversion wave: red dots turning yellow (capped), then green (producing). It is an illustration, not a forecast. In 2026 the Oklahoma House voted 85 to 6 for a bill to let old wells be turned into geothermal energy (HB 3173). The Senate did not take it up before its deadline, and it can be filed again in 2027.
