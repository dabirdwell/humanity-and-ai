## From Liability to Asset

Oklahoma has more than 20,000 known abandoned oil and gas wells that still need to be plugged or taken over: 20,406 in the [January 2025 report](https://oklahoma.gov/content/dam/ok/en/occ/documents/og/OCC-Plan-Addressing-Orphaned-Well-Plugging-Challenges.pdf) the Oklahoma Corporation Commission, the state agency that regulates oil and gas, gave a state Senate subcommittee. The Commission says the real number is almost certainly higher, because many early wells were never recorded. Each one is an environmental hazard, and with no company left to plug it, the cost falls to the state.

Phoenix Wells is working to turn these liabilities into clean energy sites: built locally, by the same skilled workforce that drilled the wells in the first place, and designed so the communities that host the work share in the value it creates.

## The Record

HB 3173, the Well Repurposing Act, passed the Oklahoma House 85–6 with bipartisan co-authors, then died at the Senate's session deadline. The coalition is proven; the framework isn't going away.

We applied to the U.S. Department of Energy, by its May 14, 2026 deadline, for funding to explore and confirm the heat underground in Oklahoma's old wells (funding opportunity DE-FOA-0003472, Topic 6). As of October 26, 2026, the application is still under review, and we will post the answer here when it comes.
