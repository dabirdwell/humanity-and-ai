Source: Economic Policy Institute, The Productivity–Pay Gap, updated September 14, 2026, with data through the middle of 2026. The top line is net productivity: everything the economy produces, minus wear on equipment, per hour worked. The bottom line is the hourly pay, wages plus benefits, of production and nonsupervisory workers (people who do not supervise others) in the private sector. Both are adjusted for inflation the same way and start at 100 in 1948, so their growth can be compared directly. The dotted line marks the end of 1979, when the two began to split. The shaded area is wealth that went somewhere other than workers' paychecks.
