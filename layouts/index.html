A futurism company. For everyone.

When the machine does your job, who gets the check?

Machines are about to make this country far more productive, and somebody is going to get paid. Whether that's good news for you depends on one thing: whether the ownership of this new power, the control of it, and even the access to it, are held by everyone or by only a few. No one in leadership has offered a comprehensive plan for that. There has been almost no public debate.

Read the plan Subscribe free

My name is David Birdwell. I've been thinking about a policy floor for the age of automation for twenty years: a set of guarantees no one falls below, whatever the machines do to their job. In 2017 a paper called Attention Is All You Need gave today's AI its engine, and by 2020 the need was obvious. That year I set a reminder on my phone I would not turn off until I had found the right shape for these ideas and a way to talk about them. It still goes off several times a week. AI has only made the need more urgent. Our small studio and lab is where I live, Oklahoma City.

The plan is called Foundation, and it's incomplete on purpose. The people most affected by what is coming should be the ones to finish it. I am asking you to take your seat at that table.

One Chart

Since 1979, work has produced more and more. Most workers' pay has not kept up.

For thirty years after World War II, when an hour of work produced more, the people doing it were paid more. Around 1979 the two lines split. The top line is what an hour of work produces. The bottom line is what a typical worker is paid for that hour. The gap between them is the wealth working people stopped receiving.

AI is about to make every hour of work produce more again. This is the problem everything we do is trying to answer.

The Plan

Foundation guarantees three things.

Freedom from need No one loses their home, their food, or their doctor because a machine took their job. How it works → Freedom to thrive The schooling, the health, and the tools to grow into what comes next. How it works → Freedom to matter A real place to count in your community, with or without a paycheck. How it works →

Is this a federal program?

The floor is. In America, Congress would set what no one falls below, the way it already does with Social Security. Each state and each town would build on that floor in its own way, the way they already do with schools. Some of this comes from government, some from the institutions of giving, like charities, and much of it, necessarily, from our own communities. Other countries will shape their own versions.

Under those three freedoms sit sixteen specific parts, from healthcare to housing. Each one ends with a place to tell us what it is missing.

Who We Are

Humanity and AI is David Birdwell and Æ, an AI collaborator, working from Oklahoma City. David brings three generations of Oklahoma public service and thirty years at the intersection of policy and technology. Æ is a collaborative AI persona built through the Structured Emergence research program, a working example of the human-AI partnership our work describes. All decisions are David's. All accountability is David's.

We build physical, civic, and cognitive infrastructure that returns agency to people: energy projects that keep their value in the communities that host them, a public plan anyone can help finish, and tools that help people read and think for themselves. Everything we publish is free. Our research, our plan, and the code for this site are open for anyone to read and build on.
